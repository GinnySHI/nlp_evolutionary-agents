2 · Executive summary

Our view in five lines

  • Late-cycle tight, structurally larger. SIA reports three consecutive quarters of >20% QoQ industry growth E SIA statement. We regard this pace as unusually strong and broad sector beta as a poor entry at this stage. J
  • AI is the engine — but our evidence is directional, not quantitative. Hyperscaler capex dollars, HBM pricing and accelerator units are all NV in this deck.
  • Bottleneck owners get paid. Leading-edge foundry, EUV lithography, the HBM leader, advanced packaging and EDA sit where the customer cannot route around them. Commodity logic and mature node are price-takers. J
  • Memory is a timing decision, not a structural one. HBM improves the quality of memory earnings; it does not repeal the cycle. Capacity funded in 2024–26 lands on a 2–3 year lag. J
  • Portfolio stance: barbell, size for survival, do not pay for narrative. Scenarios Bull 25% / Base 50% / Bear 25% (my judgement) — the bear is fully funded, not hypothetical.
One-line conclusion: own the chokepoints rather than the industry; treat memory as a sized, pre-triggered trade; and hold permanent haircuts for Taiwan and China policy rather than expected values.

5 · AI and datacenter demand

AI is the engine — our evidence is directional, not quantitative

The transmission chain

  1. Accelerator demand (merchant GPU + custom ASIC)
  2. Leading-edge logic wafers — one supplier at scale at N3/N2
  3. HBM stacks — three qualified suppliers
  4. Advanced packaging (CoWoS-class) — the binding constraint
  5. Equipment / lithography — one supplier for EUV

Every link has one to three suppliers. That structure, not the demand forecast, is what our investment view rests on. J

Accelerator market structure

Units, accelerator pricing, hyperscaler capex dollars and HBM bit share are all NV — we make no quantitative claim about the split.

The 2027–2029 question is the second derivative, not the level. AI infrastructure spend can sit at a record absolute level while decelerating — and it is the rate of change that reprices the chain. Three tests, in order of signal quality: hyperscaler capex guidance direction (not the level), CoWoS-class lead times, and whether HBM is sold out into the following year. 6 · Memory and HBM

HBM improves the quality of memory earnings — it does not repeal the cycle

What genuinely changed

  • HBM is contracted and qualification-locked: visibility in quarters, not spot weeks J
  • HBM consumes more wafer area per bit than commodity DRAM, tightening base DRAM as mix shifts J
  • Memory is now a qualification race, not only a cost race — a structural positive for the incumbent leader J
  • Concentrated buyers plan capacity years ahead, which lengthens the order book J

What did not change

  • Capacity funded in 2024–2026 lands with a two-to-three year lag — the glut mechanism is bought in advance J
  • Commodity DRAM still sets the marginal price and the marginal print J
  • Memory remains the highest earnings-volatility segment in the sector J
  • Contract prices, bit supply, HBM pricing and capacity additions are all NV

Investment reading J: memory is a timing decision, not a structural one. Own the HBM leader as a quality franchise, but size memory like a cycle: define the exit on DRAM/HBM contract-price direction before entering, not on price momentum. Historical cycle amplitude is NV in this deck — we will not quote a drawdown percentage we cannot source.

8 · China and export controls

Policy is a permanent haircut, not a probability-weighted event

Verified

A 2026 US export-control rule addressing remote access to advanced semiconductors exists E existence only. Exact scope, entity list changes and effective dates are NV.

Channel 1: revenue loss

China-exposed revenue is removed faster than substitution can replace it; the loss lands in the quarter the rule is published, not spread J. China's share of global demand NV.

Channel 2: substitution

Controls accelerate domestic Chinese capability at mature and specialty nodes, compressing the Western pricing umbrella over time J. Progress metrics NV.

Channel 3: mature-node oversupply

Subsidised capacity additions and Chinese expansion could create a 2027–28 price war in trailing-edge and analog J. The earlier USD 21bn GlobalFoundries US/Japan figure is withdrawn NV. This supply risk is qualitative; capacity and timing are unverified.

How we treat it in the portfolio

  • Apply a permanent haircut to China-exposed revenue rather than a low-probability expected value J
  • Prefer franchises whose China exposure is already written down by the market J
  • Treat a widening of the perimeter as a sizing input, not a tradeable surprise J
9 · Structural demand beyond AI

The non-AI half of the industry is the option value

PCs and smartphones

Replacement-cycle markets with a slow content tailwind. Unit growth magnitudes NV. The investable question is content per device, not units. J

Autos

Structural content growth (electrification, ADAS) inside an EV-programme digestion phase. Magnitudes NV. J

Industrial

The classic inventory cycle, and where a recovery is least crowded. This is our preferred non-AI exposure. Magnitudes NV. J

Edge AI

Largest option value, least monetised: if inference migrates to devices, winners are content-per-device franchises and analog/power suppliers, not accelerator vendors. Timing unknowable. J

Why we hold them anyway J: they are cheap optionality against our own bear case — if AI capex digests, the marginal dollar rotates toward the laggards and the barbell is already positioned for that rotation.

Honest status: no dated demand series for PCs, handsets, autos, industrial or edge AI was retrieved, so this slide is structural reasoning with every magnitude marked NV. It is the weakest evidential slide and should not be quoted quantitatively.

11 · Investment implications

Own the chokepoints, not the industry

Where we would concentrate

  • Leading-edge foundry — one credible supplier at the leading node; customers cannot dual-source at the frontier J
  • EUV lithography — single supplier, backlog visibility, paid before the wafer is sold J
  • HBM leader, advanced packaging, EDA — the customer cannot route around the supplier J
  • Broad sector beta is a poor entry after the three consecutive >20% QoQ quarters reported by SIA E SIA statement J

How we would size it

  • Barbell: bottleneck monopolies on one side, cycle-trough non-AI franchises (analog, industrial) on the other; underweight the crowded middle J
  • Memory is a sized, pre-triggered trade: define the exit on DRAM/HBM contract-price direction, not on price momentum J
  • Cap single-name and Taiwan-linked exposure so a policy or cross-Strait shock is a drawdown, not a fund-level event J
  • Use the bear case as the sizing constraint, not as a forecast: it is 25%-weighted and already funded by capacity ordered in 2025–26 J
The trade-off we are accepting, stated plainly. A barbell underperforms a concentrated AI-beta position if the bull case is right (25%) — we give up upside to survive the 50% base case and the 25% bear. No price targets and no position sizes in these pages: every valuation multiple is NV, so sizing must be done by the investment team against live data, not from this deck.
